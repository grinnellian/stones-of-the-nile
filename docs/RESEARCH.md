## Quarry Sources

- **Local Giza limestone** — bulk core blocks, quarried immediately south/west of the pyramid. Nummulitic limestone, soft, workable with copper tools. Cut using copper chisels and wooden wedges (wetted to expand and crack).
- **Tura** — Fine white casing limestone, east bank of Nile, 13-17km south. 67,000-115,000 casing blocks, avg 2.5t, some up to 15t. Transported by barge.
- **Aswan** — Red granite, 934km upstream. King's Chamber, relieving chambers, sarcophagus. 25-80 ton blocks. Quarried with dolerite pounders (copper too soft). Transported by barge during high flood.
- **Fayum** — Basalt for temple floor.
- **Hatnub** — Alabaster (calcite), Middle Egypt.
- **Sinai** — Copper for tools.
- **Lebanon** — Cedar for boats and structural timber (imported).

## Nile Transport (Confirmed by Merer Papyri, ~2560 BCE)

- Inspector Merer's crew of ~40 men made round-trip journeys Tura→Giza by boat, several days each way
- Harbor/canal system at foot of Giza plateau connected to Nile (excavated by Lehner's team)
- Pollen core analysis (Sheisha et al., 2022) confirmed Khufu branch of Nile was navigable during 4th Dynasty
- Khufu's solar boat: 43.6m long. Cargo barges likely 20-30m.

## Seasonal Cycle

- **Akhet (Inundation, Jul-Oct)**: Nile rises 4-8m. Peak transport season (deeper water, closer to plateau). Farmers available as construction labor (fields underwater). 10,000-20,000 seasonal conscripts.
- **Peret (Growing, Nov-Mar)**: Lower water. Quarrying and placement continue. Stone transport harder.
- **Shemu (Harvest, Apr-Jun)**: Lowest water. Workers needed in fields. Reduced construction workforce. Hot.

## Worker Organization (Giza Workers' Village, Lehner excavations)

- ~400m south of Sphinx, estimated 20,000-30,000 at peak
- **Hierarchy**: Crews (~2,000) → Phyles (~200) → Divisions (~20)
- **Crew names in graffiti**: "Friends of Khufu," "Drunkards of Menkaure"
- Gallery-style barracks housing 40-50 workers each
- Centrally managed bakeries (thousands of bread molds found), breweries, granaries
- Well-fed: cattle, sheep/goat, fish. Not slaves — paid laborers and corvée workers.
- Medical care: healed fractures, set bones, amputations that healed
- Burials near pyramids suggest honored status

## Construction Theory Details

### External Ramp
- Straight ramp at 7-8% gradient → 1.6km long to reach 146m summit
- Ramp volume approaches pyramid volume (~2.6M cubic meters) — central objection
- Zigzag variant: reduces length, creates turning problems for sledges
- Spiral wrap: obscures corners (can't check alignment), tight turns
- Materials: mudbrick, rubble fill, tafla clay, limestone chips. Surface wetted for sledge friction.
- Sledge teams: 20-40 men per 2.5-ton block
- **Game note**: The brute-force approach. Material logistics are the bottleneck.

### Internal Spiral Ramp (Houdin, 2007)
- Bottom third (~43m) via conventional external ramp, upper two-thirds via internal spiral ramp ~1.6m wide
- Open notches at each corner for 90-degree turns
- Supported by 1986 EDF microgravimetry survey showing spiral density anomaly
- Grand Gallery (47m long, 8.5m high) as counterweight track for 60-80 ton granite beams
- Notch at NE corner (~90m) observed by Bob Brier and Houdin, 2008
- **Game note**: The elegant architect approach. Fewer resources, more skill.

### Hydraulic/Water Theories
- Water-lubricated sledge hauling confirmed: wetting sand reduces friction ~50% (Bonn, U Amsterdam, 2014)
- Tomb of Djehutihotep painting shows water-pouring technique
- Vertical water-lift mechanisms unproven (no archaeological evidence for locks/shafts)
- Canal transport to site well-established (Merer papyri, harbor excavations)
- **Game note**: Front-loaded infrastructure investment. Nile-dependent, seasonal.

### Geopolymer/Cast-in-Place (Davidovits, 1980s)
- Blocks cast from limestone aggregate + natron + lime + kaolinite clay + water
- Barsoum (Drexel, 2006): amorphous silica and nano-spheres in samples consistent with geopolymeric synthesis
- Some blocks show air bubbles, variable density, organic fibers, wet-cast interfaces
- Critics: tool marks in quarries, quarries exist and show extraction patterns, no textual evidence
- Natron from Wadi Natrun (~100km from Giza), lime from burning limestone
- **Game note**: Completely different supply chain. Process engineering, not heavy hauling. Workers carry 20-30kg baskets instead of dragging 2,500kg blocks.

## Pharaoh (1999) Game Mechanics Reference

### Career Progression
Village Elder → Overseer → Mayor → Governor → Royal Architect → Pharaoh. Later missions unlock more building types, complex economies, monuments, military.

### Mission Objectives
Multi-criteria: Culture Rating, Prosperity Rating, Monument Rating, Kingdom Rating, Population minimum. Must satisfy ALL simultaneously. Ratings can fluctuate — must sustain them.

### Key Design Patterns
- Bazaar traders walk routes distributing goods to houses — logistics IS the puzzle
- Housing evolution requires multiple goods types (food → food+beer → food+beer+linen+luxury)
- Monument building in phases/layers over many game-years, creating sustained resource pressure
- Branching mission choices (peaceful vs military)
- Nile flooding creates annual rhythm forcing seasonal planning

### Monument Construction
- Work camps with carpenter/bricklayer/stonemason guilds near build site
- Material delivered to nearby storage yards → workers carry to monument
- Pyramids built layer by layer from bottom up
- Each layer requires materials delivered before proceeding to next
- Road layout and storage yard placement critical for throughput

## Theory E: Accretion Overbuild with Cascading Material

Recent theory (needs specific attribution — user encountered it recently) proposing:

### Core Concept
- Each pyramid built as a **massive trapezoidal overbuild** significantly larger than the final form
- Construction ramps are **integrated into the overbuild mass** — not separate disposable infrastructure
- After reaching full height, the excess is **carved down** to achieve the final smooth pyramid faces
- The removed material is **not waste** — it becomes the building material for the next structure

### The Cascading Mass Accounting
The elegant mathematical insight:
- Great Pyramid overbuild mass ≈ mass needed for Khafre's pyramid + its overbuild
- Khafre's overbuild ≈ Menkaure's pyramid + its overbuild
- Menkaure's overbuild ≈ temples, causeways, enclosure walls, mastaba fields
- **Total quarried material = total complex mass** (accounting for ~1 million tons of non-pyramid structures)
- No unexplained surplus or deficit of material

### Why This Solves Multiple Problems
1. **Ramp disposal problem**: Ramps don't need to be removed — they're carved into the pyramid face
2. **Alignment problem**: Final precision faces are cut top-down on a solid mass, not built course-by-course
3. **Material accounting**: Explains where ALL the quarried stone ended up
4. **Construction sequence**: Provides a natural ordering for the entire complex build

### Two-Phase Construction Per Structure
1. **Build-up phase**: Bulk operations. Hauling, stacking, filling. External ramp logistics for lower portion, integrated ramp for upper. Large unskilled workforce.
2. **Carve-down phase**: Precision operations. Skilled stonecutters working top-down, removing excess to reveal final faces. Generates massive material output. Smaller but highly skilled workforce.

### Implications for Game Design
- Transforms the complex from isolated projects into a **directed acyclic graph** of material flow
- Every structure is both a **consumer** (during build-up) and a **producer** (during carve-down)
- Overbuild decisions propagate: more overbuild = easier ramps but more carving + more downstream material
- The carve-down → next-structure pipeline creates natural multi-site gameplay
- Timing coordination across the complex is the core challenge
